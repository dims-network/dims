# Upgrading to v2.0.0 — what it actually took

Written **during** the upgrade, not after it. The useful part of a migration
record is the friction, and friction is the first thing a retrospective forgets:
the step that needed a second attempt, the number that was not what I expected,
the thing the tooling should have said and did not.

Every "what the tooling should have done" below is a candidate issue against the
core, not a note about the study it happened in.

> Status: in progress. Sections are filled in as each study is upgraded.

## What v2.0.0 changes for a study

The payload format, and all three cross-wavelet significance levels. Both mean
**every RQA, cross-RQA and cross-wavelet output must be recomputed** — a study
that bumps `dimsCore` and does not rebuild gets a message in every analysis tab
saying so, which is itself new in this release.

The full list is in [`CHANGELOG.md`](../../CHANGELOG.md). This file is about
what happened when the three studies actually took it.

## Before any study was touched

Two gaps found by surveying the studies rather than by upgrading one, and both
are now checks rather than paragraphs. Worth recording because the survey took
twenty minutes and each would have cost an afternoon to diagnose from the other
end.

**Nothing compared a study's committed assets against the core it pins.** The
release note says every output must be recomputed; a study that bumps and does
not is exactly the state that produces empty tabs, and the only place it
surfaced was a browser. `dims-case check` now fails on it, naming the file and
the command.

**A study-owned tab could shadow a built-in in silence.** `case-karnatak` has a
`tabs/network.js` whose id v2.0.0's built-in claims. `registerTab` refuses the
duplicate with a `console.error`, so the study keeps a file that does nothing.
`check` warns, and `sync` says it at bump time.

> **For next time.** Both were found by asking "what will each existing study
> hit?" before publishing, not after. That question is cheap and belongs in
> every release, before the tag.

## A flaky test in the gate

`test_json_carries_no_bare_nan` asserted `"NaN" not in json.dumps(block)`. Since
v2.0.0 a block contains base64 payloads, and the letters N, a and n turn up in
base64 by chance. Measured: **5 failures in 400 runs**, none of them about
anything the test exists to check.

It surfaced only when the suites were run together — one directory at a time,
the odds are low enough to look like noise.

> **For next time.** A flaky test in a gate is worse than no test: it teaches
> people that red means "run it again". When an assertion greps serialised
> output, it has to exclude anything opaque, or it is a coin toss.

## The upgrade, per study

### `case-demo` and `case-ortho`

Both took `dims-case sync . --version 2.0.0` and a rebuild, and **every asset
checksum came out unchanged** — 24 s for ORTHO's twelve recordings, seconds for
demo. That is the answer to the question worth asking about a release that moves
a format: the recompute reproduced the output committed earlier at 2.0.0 byte
for byte, so reading `analysis.rqa` and `analysis.crqa`, which neither study
sets, moved nothing.

Only `MANIFEST.json` differed, by its `generated` timestamp.

> **For next time.** The manifest's timestamp means `--write-manifest` always
> produces a diff, even when every checksum matches. A study re-running it sees
> "assets changed" in `git status` when they did not. Worth dropping, or
> recording alongside the checksums rather than as part of them.

### `case-karnatak`

The only study with genuinely new behaviour, and the one that found two defects.

`dims-case sync` reported the shadowed tab **at bump time**, which is the check
written two hours earlier for exactly this case:

```
karnatak: 1.5.1 -> 2.0.0
  note: tabs/network.js registers the tab id 'network', which the built-in
        vendor/dims-tabs/network.js also claims...
```

Deleting that tab is what v2.0.0 asks for, and doing it exposed the first
defect: **`index.html` kept the `<script src="tabs/network.js">` tag**, because
`_register_study_tabs` returned early on an empty `tabs/` and left the previous
list in place. A 404 on every page load. Fixed in v2.0.1.

The study's own tab hard-coded two people and their colours; the built-in takes
them from config, so the upgrade writes them there:

```json
"include_network": {
  "groups": [{"label": "Teacher", "match": "^teacher", "color": "#e84393"},
             {"label": "Student", "match": "^student", "color": "#00b894"}],
  "band": [0.0, 12.0]
}
```

Without that, every measure lands in one column and the bump is a visible
regression. Nothing in the CHANGELOG could have told the owner the *colours*;
what it can say, and now does, is that the grouping has to move into config.

#### The cost, measured — and my estimate was wrong

One cross-wavelet pair with a 100-surrogate coherence null:

| recording | samples | time |
|---|---|---|
| `..._2min` | 6,000 | 47 s |
| `..._for_DIMS` | 58,700 | **49 s** |

I predicted 7.7 minutes for the long one by scaling the short one with N, and
was nine times out. The Monte Carlo null's cost is set by the **number of
scales**, not the length of the recording — 99 scales in both, because this
study caps `maxPeriod` at 12 s. Verified that the second run really computed
rather than reusing the cache.

> **For next time.** The "~2.8 hours for ORTHO" figure that made this look
> frightening is about ORTHO's *pair count*, not its recording lengths. A study
> planning a run should multiply pairs by recordings, not by minutes of video —
> and `maxPeriod` is the setting that actually moves the cost.

The full recompute — 30 pairs across both recordings, 100 surrogates throughout,
ten cores — took **255 seconds**. The browser payload for the long recording
went from 35 MB to 20 MB.

#### Two things the upgrade left behind

**`_full.json` is 1.4 GB** for the long recording: 15 pairs × 99 scales ×
58,700 times × three grids, as base64 float32. The `.npz` it replaces was
966 MB, so the 1.8× the contract predicted came out at 1.47× here. It is outside
the repository and the study's `.gitignore` keeps it there, but it is written on
every rebuild.

**The old `.npz` files are still on disk** — another 1.06 GB across the two
recordings. v2.0.0 stopped writing them and nothing removes or mentions the ones
already there.

> **For next time.** Two candidate issues, both about a study's data folder
> rather than its repository, which is why neither shows up in any check we
> have. `dims-case check` could name orphaned `.npz` files after an upgrade; and
> `saveFullResolution` deserves a word in the CHANGELOG about what it costs on a
> study this size, because "1.8× larger than the archive" reads very differently
> at 4 MB and at a gigabyte.

## What v2.0.0 got wrong

A release is the first time a contract meets a study it did not have in mind.
Three things surfaced, and two of them were mine rather than the software's.

**A deleted study-owned tab kept its `<script>` tag.** The migration v2.0.0 asks
for — delete `tabs/network.js`, the network is a built-in now — left `index.html`
pointing at a file that was gone. `_register_study_tabs` generates that list from
the directory, and an empty directory made it keep the previous list rather than
write an empty one. Shipped as v2.0.1, found on the first study to try it.

**996 staged files reached v2.0.0's commits.** `apps/builder/.gitignore` still
named `app/_staging/`, the path from before the package was renamed, so the
pattern matched nothing and `git add -A` swept the wizard's staging directory
into five commits. Checked before reacting: 12 distinct blobs behind those paths,
9 of them byte-identical to files already tracked in `apps/builder/samples/`, and
`.git` is 9.8 MB — the repository grew by almost nothing, so rewriting a
published tag would have been out of all proportion. Untracked again in v2.0.1,
along with two sibling patterns: `app/_state.json`, which named a file the
builder does not write, and an unanchored `test/` that would have silently
swallowed any directory of that name.

**A test in the gate could fail by coincidence**, as recorded above.

> **For next time.** Every one of these was found by *doing* the migration, not
> by reading about it. The two checks added before publishing were found by
> asking "what will each existing study hit?"; the three above needed the studies
> themselves. Both halves are cheap. Neither is optional.

## v2.1.0 — a feature removed in a rewrite, found by the person who used it

The migration's last finding arrived as a screenshot, two days after the tag:
two columns of dots joined by grey dashes, with a thick black bar down each
column, where the study used to have a drawing of two people.

Making the cross-effector network a built-in meant rewriting it generically, and
I cut 1245 lines to 400. Everything I cut was a feature: the body silhouettes,
bowed edges, click-an-edge-for-its-detail, the width sensitivity, the period
band, the pair list, the scope control, the movement context, the explanation.
The tab still drew *something*, so nothing failed.

One of the two visible faults was not a missing feature at all but a plain bug I
introduced: collinear nodes joined by straight lines are the same line, drawn
three times. The original had already solved it and said so in a comment I read
while porting:

> Every edge is bowed perpendicular to its chord by an amount unique to its
> rank, so edges that would otherwise overlap (e.g. the collinear hand row) fan
> out into distinguishable arcs.

> **For next time.** Every test I wrote for that tab asked whether it drew
> *something* — edges exist, groups are labelled, an at-chance edge is dashed.
> None asked whether two edges were the same line, because I was testing what I
> had written rather than what the thing was for. When a rewrite replaces
> something that worked, the tests worth having are the ones the old version
> would have failed: **list what it does before you cut, and make each item a
> test.** A screenshot from a user is a slow, expensive way to learn that a
> control is gone.

> **And the smaller lesson.** The comment explaining the bug was in the file I
> was porting from, above the function I chose not to port. Reading is not the
> same as acting; when the source of a rewrite explains why something is the way
> it is, that explanation is a requirement.

## The shape of the next release note

What the CHANGELOG could not have told a study owner, and what it should:

- **that the grouping had to move into config.** It said the network became a
  built-in and that a study must delete its own copy. It did not say that the
  built-in reads its groups from `include_network`, so a study that deleted the
  tab and stopped there would have got one undifferentiated column of nodes and
  no idea why.
- **what a recompute costs, in the units that decide it.** Pairs × recordings ×
  the scale count, not minutes of video.
- **what a rebuild leaves behind.** Orphaned artifacts from the previous format.
